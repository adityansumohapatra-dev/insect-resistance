The Problem

Invasive insects and agricultural pests cause over $70 billion in damages annually. To combat them, we rely heavily on pesticides, but pests rapidly evolve resistance.

In entomology, there are two types of resistance: physiological (the insect survives the toxin) and behavioral (the insect alters its behavior to avoid the toxin entirely). Unfortunately, standard 24-hour survival tests and Y-tube olfactometers only capture binary "survive/die" outcomes. They completely miss the subtle, microscopic hesitation and avoidance behaviors that characterize early-onset behavioral resistance.

The Solution

We built a fully auditable classical computer vision and kinematics pipeline to track insects in a dual-choice leaf-disc assay. No black-box Deep Learning is used.

By extracting raw tracking data (using OpenCV MOG2 and SORT) and passing it through a rigid mathematical physics engine, every single metric—from velocity to sharp turns—is completely explainable. We translate microscopic biological hesitation into hard, quantified data.

Methodology (The Math)

Our scoring mechanism isolates physical hesitation using the following classical kinematic metrics:

  • Velocity (\(v_t\)): Discrete derivative of position. \( v_t = \frac{\sqrt{(x_t - x_{t-1})^2 + (y_t - y_{t-1})^2}}{\Delta t} \)
  • Deceleration (\(a_t\)): Sudden braking is the primary biomechanical indicator of hesitation.
  • Turning Angle (\(\theta_t\)): Change in heading between frames. Large angles indicate sharp reorientation away from a stimulus.
  • Tortuosity (\(\tau\)): The localized "squiggliness" of the path over a sliding window, calculated as path length over chord length (\( \frac{L}{C} \)).
